The Disclosure Principle — Why This System Can Be Honest

Most EdTech fails legally by obscuring what it does. It calls behavioral tracking "personalization." It describes cognitive profiling as "adaptive learning." It buries what the system actually is in terms of service no one reads. Then it is surprised when regulators and parents object. Constellation does not have this problem — because the system's stated philosophy and its legal obligations point in exactly the same direction: tell the truth about what this is.

The honest description: Constellation is a competitive multiplayer strategy game — genuinely one of the most variable and cognitively demanding in its class — with a cognitive development system built underneath it. The game is exceptional on its own terms. The meta-system makes it something more. Both are real. Stating both plainly is the philosophical commitment and the legal requirement simultaneously.

The Four Legal Regimes That Apply

COPPA US · Under 13

No personal information collected from under-13 users without verifiable parental consent. Minimum age gate is the primary mechanism. If 13+ is strictly enforced: COPPA does not apply. If any under-13 users exist: full COPPA compliance required.

FERPA US · School-Deployed

If a school enrolls students, the school holds the consent rights. A signed DPA with the school satisfies FERPA and covers parental consent. This is the simplest legal path for institutional deployment.

GDPR + UK Children's Code EU/UK

The UK Children's Code applies to any service "likely to be accessed by children under 18" — not just services marketed to them. Requires privacy by default, no profiling for non-obvious purposes, explicit consent for cognitive assessment data. Most demanding regime.

US State Laws Consumer Deployment

SOPIPA (CA, CO, NY, and many others): do not sell student data, do not use it for advertising, provide deletion on request. Your architecture already satisfies the spirit of all of these — it just needs to be stated.

The brain metrics integration (Phase 2 / BT Bridge) requires a separate consent layer. When cognitive assessment data from a validated external instrument is added to the ledger, that data may qualify as health or psychological data under GDPR and US state law — requiring explicit, separate consent, not just disclosure. Do not let Phase 2 data inherit the existing consent structure silently. Flag it in a pre-registered refutation condition now: current consent covers game performance signals; transfer_elo and brain_metrics require new consent when introduced.

What Constellation Is

Constellation is a competitive multiplayer strategy game — one of the most configurationally variable and cognitively demanding in its class. The same rules, the same map, the same moves produce genuinely different cognitive experiences depending on four parameters. That flexibility is the design.

Built underneath the game is a cognitive development system. It observes how you play, builds a model of your strengths and development areas across six cognitive dimensions, and uses that model to design better sessions. The game and the system are both what Constellation is.

This policy describes what the system records, why, who can see it, and what rights you have over it.

What Is Recorded

Every session generates a record in the system's ledger. The ledger contains:

  • Session configuration: the multiplier settings, map, round length, number of rounds, and mode that defined the session
  • Outcomes: your final rank among players, your score, your team's result
  • Behavioral signals: round-by-round performance, move timing, strategy adaptation patterns, and similar in-session signals
  • Session metadata: the date and time of the session, the session identifier
  • A student identifier: a code that links records to you without storing your name in the ledger itself

The ledger does not contain your name, your school, your device information, or any information about you outside the game.

What Is Derived From What Is Recorded

From the ledger, the system derives a model of your cognitive performance across six areas: spatial reasoning, network thinking, goal-directed search, inhibitory control, cognitive flexibility, and resource optimization. This model is what the system uses to select your next session.

The model is a view — a current approximation built from observations, not a permanent label. Every value in it has a refutation condition: if the data says otherwise, the model updates.

What Is Not Recorded

  • Your real name (unless you have confirmed your identity at the ALPHA tier — see below)
  • Your location
  • Your device, browser, or IP address beyond what is required for the network connection
  • Any communication between players
  • Any information about you from outside Constellation

Who Can See Your Data

How Long Data Is Kept

Ledger records are kept for the duration of your enrollment plus three years, or until you request deletion — whichever comes first. Derived model values (mode Elo, velocity, zone history) are deleted when the ledger records they are derived from are deleted.

If your school's program ends, the school administrator has 90 days to request an export before records are deleted on schedule.

Your Rights

  • See your data. You can request a full export of your ledger records and derived model at any time.
  • Delete your data. You can request complete deletion of your ledger and all derived values. Deletion is permanent. Sessions recorded before deletion cannot be recovered.
  • Correct your identifier. If your student identifier is incorrect, contact your administrator.
  • EU/UK users: You additionally have the right to object to processing, to restrict processing, and to data portability. Your data is processed under the lawful basis of legitimate educational interest. You may withdraw consent at any time by requesting deletion.

Users Under 13

Constellation requires users to be 13 or older to participate without parental consent. If you are under 13, a parent or guardian must complete the consent form before you can participate. If we learn that a student under 13 has participated without parental consent, we will delete their records immediately.

Younger Users — EU and UK

In the European Union and United Kingdom, the minimum age for processing personal data without parental consent varies by country (13–16). If your country requires parental consent for users under 16, that consent is required before participation. Your school's enrollment process confirms this consent has been obtained.

Future Data Types

If the system adds new categories of data — including data from external cognitive assessment tools — that expansion will require new explicit consent from you (and your parent or guardian if applicable) before collection begins. You will not be opted in automatically.

Contact and Deletion Requests

To request a data export or deletion, or for any questions about this policy: contact your program administrator, or reach the system operator directly at the address provided by your school. Requests are processed within 30 days.

Deletion Flow — What Happens When a Student or Parent Requests Deletion

The deletion flow must be documented, implemented, and communicated. These are the steps, the student-facing confirmation screen, and the terminal-voice version of the deletion receipt.

Process — What Actually Happens on Deletion
1
Request received
Student, parent, or teacher submits deletion request. Request is logged with timestamp and requester identity. Processing begins within 24 hours.
2
Ledger records removed
All JSONL records for this student identifier are deleted from the ledger files. The append-only nature of the ledger does not exempt records from deletion — a deletion marker is appended and the record is excluded from all reads, with physical removal at the next scheduled purge (within 30 days).
3
Derived model cleared
All Layer 1 state for this student — mode Elo values, velocity, ZPD targets, zone history, session count — is deleted. These are views derived from Layer 0; they cannot persist after Layer 0 is removed.
4
Student identifier unlinked
The mapping from the student's real identity (if confirmed) to their ledger hash is deleted. After this step, the hash is an orphan — even if a record remained, it could not be attributed to the student.
5
Confirmation sent
Deletion receipt sent to the requester (student email or parent email). The receipt states what was deleted and confirms completion. No records remain.

One legal subtlety about the append-only ledger: GDPR Article 17 and COPPA both require actual deletion, not just flagging.
