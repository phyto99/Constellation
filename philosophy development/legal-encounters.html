Why This Exists — The Philosophical Necessity of the Data and the Mystery

// the honest version The data collection is not a legal requirement layered on top of a game. It is the system. Without it, there is no system — only a game. The opacity is not a commercial convenience. It is the mechanism that makes the development work. Both are explained here. Completely.

I. Why the Recording Is the System, Not Incidental to It

The One Principle of this system is: maximize expected information gain about the student's cognitive model per session. Written formally: argmax E[KL(posterior ‖ prior)].

That principle requires two things to exist. A prior — what the system currently believes about a student's cognitive profile. And a posterior — what the system believes after observing the session. The gap between them is the information gain. The session that maximizes that gap is the right next session for this student.

The ledger is the prior. Without the ledger, there is no prior. Without the prior, there is no posterior. Without the posterior-prior gap, the system cannot compute which session will teach this student the most. The system becomes a random session generator — one that ignores everything it has already learned about this student and starts over every time. The data collection is not a feature of the system. It is the implementation of the One Principle. Removing it removes the system.

This is structurally different from why other systems collect data. Social media collects behavioral data to maximize time on platform — an objective that is directly opposed to the user's long-term interest. The ledger exists to maximize the rate at which the student develops past needing the system. The objective the data serves is the student's cognitive development — nothing else. When the student no longer needs the system, the system has succeeded. Social media calls that failure.

II. Why the Mystery Is Load-Bearing

The system withholds one thing: the specific formula — the weights, the Elo constants, the threshold values, the exact algorithm that maps behavioral signals to cognitive estimates. This is the only opacity in the system. Everything else is disclosed or disclosed progressively. The reason for this specific opacity is not commercial. It is developmental.

The second game. The terminal gives students observations after every session. The observations are specific and true — but they never say "this is what we are measuring" or "this is how the formula works." Students begin to notice patterns. They compare terminal messages with classmates. They form theories about what the system is tracking. They test those theories against the next session's output. This process — abductive reasoning applied to an opaque system — is itself a cognitive development exercise. The student who has reverse-engineered the system's measurement framework has demonstrated exactly the kind of abstract analytical thinking the system is designed to develop.

If the formula were disclosed, the second game would not exist. A student could simply read the formula, verify that their behavior matched it, and confirm or disconfirm the model. That is a very different cognitive task — and a far less demanding one — than constructing a working model of the system from observations alone. The opacity creates a specific kind of productive uncertainty. That uncertainty is what the system develops.

This is different from social media opacity in a precise way: social media hides its algorithm because disclosure would reveal that it is optimizing against the user's interests. Constellation partially withholds its algorithm because full disclosure would eliminate the mechanism that makes the development work. The reasons are opposite. The first is self-protective. The second is self-defeating — explaining it loses the effect it produces, and this document is explaining it anyway, because that is what honesty requires.

The opacity has a limit. The system is opaque about the formula. It is not opaque about anything else. The existence of the model, the categories of data it uses, the six dimensions it estimates, the confidence intervals it maintains, the refutation conditions on every parameter, the fact that all parameters are currently ASSERTED and will be replaced by measured values — all of that is disclosed. The formula is the only exception, and the reason for the exception is stated here plainly. That is the maximum transparency the system can offer without destroying what makes it work.

III. Why Mystery and Trust Are Not Opposites

Trust is not produced by complete information. It is produced by accurate information about what the system is doing and why. A system that discloses everything about its mechanics but pursues objectives that conflict with the user's interest is not trustworthy. A system that withholds one thing for a stated reason that serves the user's interest is.

The students who have played Constellation longest trust the system most — not because they have more information about the formula, but because the observations the terminal gives them have turned out to be accurate over time. The model's predictions about what mode they should develop next have, when tested, been right more often than their own predictions. Trust was built through demonstrated accuracy, not through disclosure of the algorithm that produced it.

The terminal's language is not designed to be soothing or encouraging. It is designed to be specific and true. "The system is not finished with this challenge. ECHO configuration active." — that is a statement of what the model computed. It is either right or wrong. Over enough sessions, students learn whether it tends to be right. That is the basis of trust: not disclosure of the mechanism, but track record of the output.

The One Exception — With Its Reason
The specific formula. The weights, the Elo constants, the threshold values that determine session selection. These are inside the system. They are not disclosed here or anywhere in the product.

The reason: The second game — figuring out what the system is measuring and why — is itself the most cognitively demanding exercise the system offers. A student who has reverse-engineered the system's measurement framework has demonstrated abstract analytical reasoning of a higher order than any session can produce directly. Disclosing the formula eliminates this exercise permanently. That is an honest developmental reason for a specific, bounded opacity. It is not self-protection. It is not commercial convenience. It is what the LILA framework calls a structural necessity — the opacity is not incentivized. It is required. Remove it and the second game cannot exist.

The limit of this exception: The exception covers the formula. It does not cover the existence of a model, the categories of data it uses, what it estimates, who sees it, how long it is kept, or the right to delete it. Every one of those things is disclosed. The formula is the only exception and this is its complete explanation.

V. Why This Document Exists

Most privacy policies exist to satisfy a legal requirement. This document exists for a different reason: the system's philosophy requires it. A system whose purpose is cognitive development through honest encounters cannot conduct its consent process dishonestly. The consent encounter is the first interaction the student has with the system. If it is a wall — a checkbox designed to extract a signature without transmitting information — it contradicts what the system is for before the first session begins.

The disclosures here are not reluctant. They are the same honesty the terminal applies to session results. The system saw something and is reporting it truthfully. In this case, what it saw is itself.

The one thing plain language cannot do: replace a signed School DPA or a submitted parental consent form. The short form is disclosure — what you tell people. They are separate legal acts.

Why Plain Language Is More Legally Robust, Not Less

Courts and regulators have increasingly ruled against dense legalese — not for it. The FTC has cited confusing consent language as a COPPA violation. The ICO (UK) has rejected privacy policies as non-compliant specifically because users could not understand them. The legal risk in this space runs toward opacity, not clarity. A parent or student who can read the short form and accurately describe what they agreed to has given better consent than one who clicked through three pages of clauses they did not read. That is what regulators want to see.

The Design Principle — Consent as Encounter

Most consent flows are walls. The user is asked to acknowledge something they did not read so they can access something they want. The wall extracts a checkbox without transmitting anything. It is compliance theater. It develops nothing and discloses nothing. This is the wrong design.

Applied to legal consent, the LILA framework produces a different object: a consent encounter. A situation where understanding what the system is is the only viable path — because the system structures the flow so that skipping produces a different, honest outcome than engaging. Not punishment. Honest differentiation.

What "behavioral signals" actually means

The ledger records more than outcome. It records the shape of how you played: how your decisions changed between round 1 and round 8, how fast you adapted when a strategy stopped working, how you distributed your attention across the board. These are the signals the model uses to distinguish game knowledge from cognitive development. A player who wins by memorizing opening sequences produces a different signal than one who adapts mid-session. The system is designed to measure the second thing, not the first.

What the model actually is

Six Elo values — one per cognitive dimension — each with a confidence interval that widens when sessions are sparse and narrows as data accumulates. Each value has a velocity measure (how fast it is changing) and a ZPD target (what the next session should aim for). Every parameter is marked ASSERTED: it is the system's current best guess, not a validated value. Phase 3 of the build plan replaces every ASSERTED parameter with a measured one. The model is honest about what it does not yet know.

Why the algorithm is not disclosed

The specific weights, the Elo formula's constants, the threshold values — these are inside the system and will not appear in this document. This is both a trade secret and a philosophical choice: the second game (figuring out what the system is measuring and why) is part of what the system develops. Disclosing the formula would collapse the second game without developing anything. You have the right to know the model exists, what categories of data it uses, and what it estimates. You do not have the right to the formula. These are legally distinct things.

The append-only ledger and deletion

The ledger is designed to be append-only — philosophically, observations are never destroyed, only added. But this design principle does not override the legal right to erasure. When deletion is requested: records are flagged immediately and excluded from all reads within 24 hours. Physical removal occurs within 30 days. Derived model values are deleted immediately. The append-only property is a performance and integrity principle; it does not mean data cannot be deleted.

What "progressive disclosure" means for your rights

At the BASELINE tier, you cannot see your model. At DEVELOPING, you see mode estimates and zone history. At ALPHA, you see the full model with confidence intervals and provenance — which raw sessions produced which estimates. This is intentional. Understanding the model is part of what the system develops. The right to access your data is unconditional — you can always request a full export. But the in-system view is progressive because the system earns your understanding of it over time.

Parent Investigation — Consent as Abductive Reasoning

The parent is not asked to sign first and understand later. They are given evidence and asked to form a view. Five phases. The designed moment fires when they submit, based on how much evidence they examined.

COGNITIVE MODE: INVESTIGATION — abductive reasoning, model construction under incomplete information. The parent's task is not verification against a known standard. It is forming an accurate model of what the system is from the evidence available. The consent is genuine only when the model is accurate.
Phase 1 — The System Declaration
What is Constellation?
Constellation is a competitive multiplayer strategy game — one of the most configurationally variable and cognitively demanding in its class — and a cognitive development system built underneath it. The game is exceptional on its own terms. The system makes it something more: an instrument that observes how your child plays, builds a model of their cognitive profile, and uses that model to give them better sessions.
Phase 2 — The Evidence (examine what you need)
What does the system actually collect? Examine the evidence.
Evidence A — What the ledger records+
Each session produces a record containing: the session configuration (mode, multiplier settings, map), your child's rank among all players in that session, round-by-round performance, and behavioral signals — how their strategy changed during play, decision timing, adaptation patterns. A code (a hash) represents your child in the ledger. The code is not their name. The ledger does not contain: name, school, location, device, IP address, or any information from outside the game.
Evidence B — What model is built from it+
Six performance estimates — one per cognitive dimension: spatial reasoning, network construction, goal-directed search, inhibitory control, cognitive flexibility, resource optimization. Each estimate has a confidence interval (how certain the system is) and a refutation condition (what data would cause it to update). These estimates are ASSERTED — the system's current best approximation, not a validated measurement. Every parameter will be revised when sufficient data exists. The model is not a diagnosis. It is a working hypothesis the system holds about your child's cognitive profile, updated every session.

The program administrator and your child's teacher see the full model. Your child sees it progressively — at the BASELINE tier (early sessions), they see nothing. At DEVELOPING, they see performance estimates and zone history. At ALPHA, they see the full model with the underlying session records that produced each estimate. No third party sees any data. The data is never sold. It is never used for advertising, research outside this program, or any purpose other than operating and improving the program.

Records are kept for the duration of enrollment plus three years, unless you request deletion earlier. You can request: a full export of all records (delivered within 15 days), deletion of all records and the derived model (completed within 30 days, permanent), or information about what the system currently holds. Deletion cannot be undone. If your child rejoins the program after deletion, the system starts with no prior knowledge of them. Contact: your program administrator, or [contact address].

Five outcome states, matching the LILA result format. Each says something true and specific. Not generic. Not a score. Something that would only be true of this particular path through this particular encounter.

LILA requirement: "Each: one heading (names what actually happened in game terms) + one paragraph (says something true, specific, not generic)." Applied here: the heading names what actually happened in the consent encounter. The paragraph says something true about that specific path.
Read Everything · Confirmed
You know what you agreed to.

Read Partially · Confirmed
You know part of what you agreed to.

Skipped Everything · Confirmed
You agreed to something you did not read.

The system will behave identically regardless.
Read Everything · Declined
You read it and said no.
That is the system working correctly. The disclosure exists so that declining is possible on an informed basis, not to produce a specific outcome. You cannot participate without agreeing to the data collection — the system cannot give you better sessions without recording what you do. If your objection is specific, contact your administrator. If it is general, the system is not the right tool for you at this time.

Graduation Condition — When Has the Consent Encounter Given Everything It Can Give?

From the LILA framework: "When has this game given everything it can give to this community? State the observable sign that no player encounters genuine uncertainty in this game anymore. State what the game leaves in the players who played it seriously — the quality that persists after the game is archived."
Observable Sign

When students arrive at session 1 already knowing that the system builds a cognitive model from gameplay signals — because the community has transmitted this knowledge before the consent encounter fires. When the consent encounter produces no new information for any incoming student because the meta-community has already made the system fully transparent. When "I know what it tracks" is the standard entry state, not the exceptional one.

Persistent Quality Left in Players

The habit of reading what they are agreeing to before agreeing to it. Not from moral pressure — from having encountered a system that responded differently based on whether they read it. The designed moment fires once. The habit, if formed, persists after the game is archived.
